About

From Sitiawan to the operating theatre

He grew up in Sitiawan, Perak, Malaysia. Today he is Head of Neurosurgery at the National University Hospital (NUH), Singapore.

He graduated from the NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine in 2007. He did basic general surgical training and then advanced neurosurgical training at NUH. An Academic Medicine Development Award in 2014 took him to Addenbrooke's Hospital, Cambridge, for two years. There he trained in paediatric neurosurgery, neuro-oncology, neurovascular, neurotrauma and complex spine surgery. He then completed a Clinical Fellowship in Paediatric Neurosurgery at The Hospital for Sick Children (SickKids), Toronto.

He leads the Adult and Paediatric NUH Brain Tumour Multidisciplinary Clinic, and he has helped set up care pathways for patients with complex brain and skull base conditions. He completed the NUS Executive MBA in 2023 and uses it in his leadership work to improve patient care, education and innovation in neurosurgery.

Expertise

Clinical focus

He operates on complex adult and paediatric cases, with a particular interest in minimally invasive brain and spine surgery. These techniques improve outcomes, speed up recovery and shorten hospital stays.

Skull base

Endoscopic pituitary & skull base surgery

He uses the endoscopic endonasal approach (EEA), which reaches tumours through the nose, for pituitary tumours, craniopharyngioma, skull base meningioma and chordoma. He trained in it through a month-long observership with EEA masters Dr Paul Gardner (UPMC) and Dr Theodore Schwartz (New York-Presbyterian).

Oncology

Surgery for brain and spine tumours, working closely with oncology colleagues at the National University Cancer Institute, Singapore.

Innovation & care models

Building better recovery

Design patent

A new burr hole cover

He holds a design patent for a burr hole cover that lets surgeons place subdural drains easily after draining a chronic subdural haematoma. Data show it improves how the scar looks, as well as patients' psychological and functional recovery.

The device has been commercialised and is available internationally.

Research

Research interests

His research aims to improve understanding and treatment of complex brain and spine tumours, especially gliomas and skull base and pituitary conditions. It sits where clinical innovation, translational science and surgical outcomes research meet.

01

Skull base & pituitary surgery

Improving microsurgical and endoscopic endonasal approaches, refining credentialing and training standards, and making surgery safer with better long-term outcomes.

02

Neuro-oncology & genomics

Studying the biology and the molecular and genetic drivers of brain tumours, including craniopharyngiomas, ependymomas and rare skull base lesions. He also works on bringing genomic tools such as long-read sequencing into treatment decisions.

03

Surgical oncology & complex resections

Finding ways to remove as much tumour as is safely possible while preserving function, in both adults and children.

04

Outcomes & health systems

Studying patient-reported outcomes, health economics and system-level changes that widen access to advanced neurosurgical care, particularly in Southeast Asia.

05

Global neurosurgery

Developing sustainable training models with regional partners, focused on local expertise in endoscopic skull base surgery and awake neurosurgery.

In the news

Stories from the operating theatre

The Straits Times · June 2024

The “miracle baby” with a brainstem tumour

Baby E was born in January 2022 with a high-grade glioma in her brainstem, a condition with odds of about one in 100 million. Her parents were told nothing could be done. At NUH, a multidisciplinary team, with Dr Nga as the neurosurgeon in charge, biopsied the tumour when she was about a week old. They worked with experts at SickKids, Toronto.

The tumour then did something unexpected: it stopped growing, and within six months it had shrunk by half. Dr Nga believes the biopsy may have triggered her immune system to fight the cancer. At two years old she was thriving, and she may be the first documented child to survive such a tumour.

“While it is not possible to be conclusive, we believe that the tumour regression, in this particular case, is related to the biopsy that was performed.”

The case was later published as “Rare regression of congenital brainstem high-grade glioma” in Child’s Nervous System.

The Straits Times · January 2026

Saving face: 20 years of craniofacial care

A young man with Crouzon syndrome has had four major operations since he was 18 months old. This rare genetic condition fuses the skull’s growth joints too early and leaves too little room for the growing brain. Adj A/Prof Nga was the neurosurgeon on the NUH team that reshaped his skull as an adult. He also monitored the patient's brain development over the years.

He explained that cutting through the skull is one of the most critical steps. The surgeon must avoid the dura, the brain’s protective outer membrane that carries major blood channels.

“Any injury to the dura or the blood channels within it could be catastrophic.”

The patient, now 23, is training for his first half-marathon.
