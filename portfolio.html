In the news

From the operating theatre

The Straits Times · June 2024

The “miracle baby” with a brainstem tumour

Baby E was born in 2022 with a high-grade glioma in her brainstem, and her parents were told nothing could be done. At NUH, with Dr Nga as the neurosurgeon in charge, the team biopsied the tumour when she was about a week old.

The tumour then stopped growing, and within six months it had shrunk by half. At two years old she was thriving, possibly the first documented child to survive such a tumour.

“We believe that the tumour regression, in this particular case, is related to the biopsy that was performed.”

Published as “Rare regression of congenital brainstem high-grade glioma”, Child’s Nervous System.

The Straits Times · January 2026

Saving face: 20 years of craniofacial care

A young man with Crouzon syndrome, a rare condition that fuses the skull’s growth joints too early, has had four major operations since he was 18 months old. Adj A/Prof Nga was the neurosurgeon on the NUH team that reshaped his skull as an adult, and he monitored his brain development over the years.

“Any injury to the dura or the blood channels within it could be catastrophic.”

The patient, now 23, is training for his first half-marathon.

About

From Sitiawan, Malaysia, to Head of Neurosurgery at NUH

He graduated from the NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine in 2007 and trained in general surgery and neurosurgery at NUH. An Academic Medicine Development Award then took him to Addenbrooke's Hospital, Cambridge, for two years, followed by a paediatric neurosurgery fellowship at The Hospital for Sick Children (SickKids), Toronto.

He leads the NUH Brain Tumour Multidisciplinary Clinic and completed the NUS Executive MBA in 2023. He played a key role in establishing the Ellen Siow Professorship in Neurosurgery, Singapore's first endowed professorship in the field, made possible by the Siow family.

Research

Research interests

His research focuses on complex brain and spine tumours, especially gliomas and skull base and pituitary conditions.

Genomics

What drives brain tumours

The molecular and genetic drivers of tumours such as craniopharyngiomas, ependymomas and rare skull base lesions, and bringing long-read sequencing into treatment decisions.

Surgical oncology

Maximal safe resection

Removing as much tumour as is safely possible while preserving function, in adults and children.

Outcomes

Access to care in Southeast Asia
